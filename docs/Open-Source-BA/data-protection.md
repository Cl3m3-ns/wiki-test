# Data Protection (GDPR)

The data protection requirements derive from the [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng). Fortunately, the AMPEL platform operates strictly locally without external data transfer. It is therefore smaller in scope regarding data protection and security concepts compared to other projects. While perhaps unfamiliar to researchers, the data is not used in a pseudonymized or anonymized form. For software applications in routine care, however, this is a necessary requirement and standard procedure. 

# Practical implementation of the requirements

- Calculate 40 working hours of staff resources.
- Conduct an evaluation with the Data Protection Officer to determine whether a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) is required (threshold analysis).
- As it involves personal / health data, the answer will likely be yes.
- Carry out the Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA).
	- Rough estimate: This will require about 5 meetings with the DPO and can be prepared in a tandem setup.
	- The risk and protection level assessment evaluates individual components. You can draw on the content provided by us for this (==Link==).
		- In accordance with the general concept, the data input module must also be created locally on your end.
- Check locally with the DPO whether anything further is required (e.g., integration into the local data protection concept).
- For German-speaking contexts, you can use the [TMF template](https://www.toolpool-gesundheitsforschung.de/produkte/template-zur-dokumentation-von-datenschutz-folgenabschaetzungen-nach-art-35-dsgvo).
- Second: contact the Information Security Officer (CISO/ISO).
- Likewise, clarify local requirements (IT security concept, structural analysis) and develop them collaboratively in tandem.
